# Tax rule source register

Reviewed and retrieved on 2026-10-12. The initial rule baseline is 2026; source publication year and applicable tax year are separate. `manifest.json` records original stored bytes, SHA-256 hashes, URLs, retrieval date, publication year, and publication date where stated. Tests compare these hashes and never fetch a live source. HTML snapshots preserve the retrieved page rather than reproducing calculator output. The text files are local PDF extractions; the PDF hash is the authoritative byte record.

Rule pack version: `us-ca-2026.1`. Every individual constant has source metadata in its `RuleValue`; resolved provenance identifies the exact field path and is aggregated only for fields a calculator actually uses. Source IDs and URLs are listed in the manifest, with full documents stored under `2026/` or `2025-reference/`.

The federal baseline follows IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 sections 4.01 (MFJ rates), 4.10 (AMT), 4.14 (standard/age deductions), and 4.26 (QBI). Public Law 119-21, enacted 2025-07-04, controls the senior deduction sunset in 2029, 50% AMT exemption phaseout from 2026, permanent acquisition debt limitation, mortgage insurance reinstatement from 2026, section 68's exact 2/37 restriction, and SALT's 1% growth through 2029 then $10,000 cap in 2030. Publication 505 (2026) confirms applicable planning amounts. Its 5.4% planning approximation does not replace the statutory 2/37 fraction.

The prior-code snapshots preserve unchanged calculation and indexing provisions. Read them together with the 2025 law; their expired standard amounts, AMT phaseouts, mortgage insurance expiry, and QBI phase-in range cannot override the 2026 law. Section 163 supplies mortgage insurance's $100,000 AGI threshold, $1,000 step (or fraction thereof), and 10% reduction. Section 199A supplies 50% wages or 25% wages plus 2.5% UBIA, and section 1(f)(7) supplies rounding. Full 2025 IRS/FTB publications are historical method evidence rather than a separate selectable baseline.

The employee payroll baseline is SSA's $184,500 contribution base, IRS Publication 15 employee rates 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare, IRS Topic 560's fixed joint $250,000 Additional Medicare threshold and 0.9% rate, and EDD's 2026 SDI 1.3% with no wage cap from 2024. EDD administers each year's SDI rate; future carryforward is explicitly projected and is never grown by an indexed-dollar assumption.

Retirement amounts follow Notice 2025-67: $24,500 elective deferral, $8,000 ordinary age-50 catch-up, $11,250 ages-60-through-63 catch-up, $7,500 IRA and $1,100 IRA age-50 catch-up. The IRS catch-up guidance and final regulations in IRB 2025-40 establish the sponsoring employer's prior-year wages, the $150,000 threshold used for 2026, Roth requirement from 2026, and inflation-increase rounding. Sections 219(g)(8) and 408A(c)(3)(D) round IRA eligibility thresholds to the NEAREST $1,000; phaseout widths remain fixed. Enhanced catch-up rounds its inflation INCREASE in $500 steps, preserving the $11,250 original amount.

California's rolling Tax News page currently contains October 2026 indexing at 3.4%, MFJ standard deduction $11,800, individual filer credit $158, and the MFJ marginal boundaries. It explicitly says complete 2026 indexed amounts will be available in late December. The dated `/2026/10.html` endpoint failed during review; the rolling page's original bytes are retained. No later publication is invented.

Some printed cumulative base taxes also differ by cents from continuous integration of prior published brackets. The usable pack explicitly assumes the published boundary $118,996 and continuous marginal integration. All bracket-boundary fields therefore remain provisional. The published standard and filer credit fields remain individually verified.

The remaining California exemption-phaseout, itemized-phaseout, and AMT thresholds are explicit provisional 2025 proxies: $504,411, $123,667 AMT exemption, and $463,745 AMT phaseout beginning. Attempts to retrieve the applicable statutory indexing endpoints failed (403 or unavailable). These amounts are not asserted to be the true 2026 indexed amounts. Removing either proxy value or its named assumption returns `needs-data`. The ordinary 6%/80% itemized reduction, $6 per exemption per $2,500 AGI step, 7% AMT, $1 million/1% high-income surtax, and $1 million acquisition debt limit have their own FTB method sources. The FTB January 2026 AB1620 analysis explicitly confirms existing California law does not allow mortgage insurance premiums; the analysis does not establish that proposed homeowner insurance relief was enacted.

Projection is a deterministic scenario. Legally indexed thresholds compound the published 2026 amounts by the user's basis-point assumption and apply the recorded rounding convention; official indices and unrounded historic bases are not predicted. Federal amounts floor where prescribed, AMT rounds to $100, IRA phaseout thresholds to nearest $1,000, and SSA to nearest $300. California uses nearest-dollar forecasting. Negative scenario growth is accepted; statutory increases-only federal amounts and SSA's no-decrease base retain the baseline. California signed indexing remains a stated projection assumption, not verified future law. Fixed thresholds/rates stay fixed and enacted transitions take priority. SALT statutory fractional dollars are settled to cents with ties away from zero; field metadata labels the legal 1% increase separately from discretionary projection.
