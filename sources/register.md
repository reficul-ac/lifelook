The federal baseline follows IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 sections 4.01 (MFJ rates), 4.10 (AMT), 4.14 (standard/age deductions), and 4.26 (QBI). The prior-code snapshots preserve unchanged calculation and indexing provisions. Read them together with the 2025 law; their expired standard amounts, AMT phaseouts, mortgage insurance expiry, and QBI phase-in range cannot override the 2026 law. Section 199A supplies 50% wages or 25% wages plus 2.5% UBIA, and section 1(f)(7) supplies rounding. Full 2025 IRS/FTB publications are historical method evidence rather than a separate selectable baseline.

The employee payroll baseline is SSA's $184,500 contribution base, IRS Publication 15 employee rates 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare, IRS Topic 560's fixed joint $250,000 Additional Medicare threshold and 0.9% rate, and EDD's 2026 SDI 1.3% with no wage cap from 2024. EDD administers each year's SDI rate; future carryforward is explicitly projected and is never grown by an indexed-dollar assumption.

Retirement amounts follow Notice 2025-67: $24,500 elective deferral, $8,000 ordinary age-50 catch-up, $11,250 ages-60-through-63 catch-up, $7,500 IRA and $1,100 IRA age-50 catch-up. ## Clean same-year backdoor Roth conversions

Reviewed October 13, 2026: [IRS Publication 590-A](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a) distinguishes conversion rollovers from annual contributions; [Form 8606 instructions (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8606) define nondeductible basis, aggregate traditional/SEP/SIMPLE IRA balances and conversion basis consumption. [FTB Publication 1005 (2025)](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-1005-publication.pdf) permits an independent California nondeductible election and documents historical federal/California basis differences. These method sources do not supply new annual numeric limits: the existing retirement rule pack supplies the applicable and projected limits.

The `clean-backdoor-roth.1` scenario elects nondeductible treatment in both books and immediately converts only the accepted same-year contribution to the same owner's Roth IRA, with no intervening growth. It requires explicit clean-history confirmation, known-zero owner opening basis, zero modeled traditional IRA balances and no other funded traditional IRA contributions that year. The confirmation covers unmodeled traditional, SEP and SIMPLE IRAs, rollovers and federal/California basis differences. Other cases remain unresolved or unsupported; taxable/pro-rata conversions are not inferred. Contributions consume the shared IRA limit once; conversions consume the corresponding new basis and transfer assets without another cash debit. Existing Publication 590-A and FTB Publication 1005 snapshots are retained; the Form 8606 snapshot and hash were added on the review date.

## RSU compensation and retained shares

The RSU scenario model `rsu-settle-at-vest.1` uses separate units, service dates, delivery, valuation and tax funding. The existing income-tax and payroll amounts retain their existing rule versions. IRS Publication5992 (revision June25,2024) distinguishes RSU vesting from share transfer and explains ordinary settlement compensation; it expressly identifies itself as audit guidance rather than a legal pronouncement. Publication15-A (2026) establishes the separate deferred-compensation FICA boundary. Publication551 (December2025) supports compensation-based stock basis. FTB Publication1004 (revised January2015) supports California equity-compensation characterization and residence allocation; its historical option examples do not supply current dollar thresholds. Original editions and hashes are retained, with the existing Publication15 and EDD wage/rate sources supplying employee payroll treatment.

The supported award delivers stock at each service vest, with compensation measured at the projected event share price. Sell-to-cover is assumed to execute immediately at that value without fees or capital gain, with fractional-share percentage allocation. Actual whole-share rounding, discretionary sales, later gains, 83(i) elections, deferred settlement and 409A failures are not inferred. Entered price growth is a forecast assumption, and entered withholding percentages are funding assumptions rather than final liability rates. Actual historical net holdings and basis are explicit opening inputs; past vesting does not create forecast cash or repeat income.
